UK Reservoirs Water Level Dashboard

Why this research matters (Dissertation Justification)

Reservoirs underpin the UK’s water security, flood risk management, energy storage, and ecosystem health. With climate variability, rapid urban growth, and aging infrastructure, decision-makers need timely, geospatial, and explainable insights into storage trends and weather impacts. This dashboard demonstrates a practical, reproducible workflow to monitor reservoirs, fuse weather history, and communicate risk clearly for policy, operations, and the public.

Climate variability & extremes Drought & flood preparedness Operational decision support Open data & transparency Public engagement Reproducible analytics
Key Research Questions
  • How can open data (reservoir metadata + historical weather) be fused to estimate storage trends when direct sensor feeds are limited?
  • Which weather drivers (rainfall, temperature) are most associated with short-term storage changes?
  • What is an effective visual narrative (maps + time series + relationships) to aid rapid situational awareness?
Objectives
  • Build an interactive geospatial interface of UK reservoirs with drill-down dashboards.
  • Integrate historical weather and compute an interpretable storage proxy (level% & m³) over time.
  • Deliver actionable visual analytics: level%, level m³, temperature trends, precipitation, and temp–rain relationship.
  • Ensure reproducibility via open sources and a transparent modelling approach.
Contributions
  • A working pipeline from web-scraped reservoir data to historical weather fusion and visual analytics.
  • A lightweight mass-balance proxy for storage that stakeholders can understand and tune.
  • A design pattern for future upgrades (IoT sensors, official level APIs, alerts, and forecasts).

Scope: demonstration with open sources; can be extended with Environment Agency feeds for live levels and alerts.
